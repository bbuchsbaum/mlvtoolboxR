# Selecting crossing members together

This development experiment asks whether a photograph supplies enough evidence to select the complete group of paths that forms an X. Use the 60 Toronto development photographs and the same five photo folds, original FDoG and M-LSD paths, and per-photo FDoG distinct-ink ceiling as the [whole-contour study](../curve-rank/PLAN.md). Refit the existing path utility model on the same 48 photos for each fold. A byte-identical replay of the previous held-out vector drawing for every photograph checks the comparator. Leave the 205 unscored photographs outside this experiment.

## Photo-only rule

In addition to each existing single-path action, allow one action to select all remaining member contours of an X from either source drawing. Use original vectors, with at least two distinct source members and four exported directions. Pay only for the union of newly inked pixels. Account for shared two-pixel neighborhoods once. Estimate the bundle's new artist coverage by multiplying the new neighborhood area (or ink, if larger) by the members' mean predicted utility density, weighted by their individual new area (or ink, if larger). Add a completion bonus up to 28 artist-pixel equivalents, corresponding to four arms spanning radii 3 through 9. The bonus is earned once for the proposed crossing. Duplicate proposals for identical remaining paths compete as alternatives. Previously taken paths cannot be selected again.

Scale the bonus by photographic support for the weakest of the four arms and by their angular separation. At radii 3, 5, 7 and 9, sample the RGB contrast between points 1.5 pixels either side of each arm. Use the mean absolute RGB channel difference, then the median along each arm, and take the weakest arm. Out-of-frame pairs contribute zero. For contrast c on the 0–255 scale, the factor is c/(c+12). Multiply by the sine of the smallest circular gap between adjacent arms. Coincident directions and zero support provide no bundle action. Choose the highest estimated gain per new ink pixel among feasible singles and groups. Ties favor larger estimated gain, then the existing single-path action, then source order. Positive-value free completions are feasible. The singleton model, paths, and scoring tolerances are fixed. Run one setting.

## Reference-assisted diagnostic

Separately, give the selector the M-LSD source X sites that actually pass one-to-one four-arm matching against the artist. Force all their member paths first if their union fits the budget, in source order, then fill with the original photo-only single-path policy. Render this drawing through the same R junction detector. This tests whether keeping the correct existing member groups can preserve crossings in a completed drawing. It provides a diagnostic achievable result on this roster. It has access to artist labels and cannot qualify the method.

## Decision

Count an X only if it matches the correct type and position within four pixels, both local sets of incident contours cover each other in the established 3–9-pixel annulus, and all four reported artist rays have a distinct matching predicted ray within 25 degrees. Enforce one-to-one matching between junctions. Report the original incidence score as well. For further confirmation require a gain of at least two four-arm X matches over the earlier selector, with gains on at least two different photos. Also require coverage at least two percentage points above FDoG, F1 within 0.005 of the earlier selector or better, at least as many positional T and X matches as the earlier selector, complete outputs and no per-photo ink overruns. Report paired category-stratified photo bootstrap intervals and actual detection counts. A failure ends this trial without using a fresh test panel.
